Why Does the House Win?

01Probability

Probability is how often an outcome happens over the long run. A 47% chance does not mean you win 47 of the next 100 games. It means the win rate settles near 47% as games pile up.

02Expected value

Expected value is the average result per game if you could play forever: each outcome times its probability, added up. Here that is p × R − (1 − p).

03House edge

The house edge is the expected loss flipped into a percentage. A 5.26% edge means the player loses about 5.26 units for every 100 units put at risk, on average.

04Short-term chaos

Over a few games, randomness is louder than the edge. Streaks happen, and plenty of players walk away ahead. Small samples swing widely.

05Long-run certainty

The law of large numbers pulls the average toward the expected value as trials increase. The spread shrinks like 1/√n, so over thousands of games the edge wins every time.
